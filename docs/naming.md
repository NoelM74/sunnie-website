# Product naming rules

Every product name follows one pattern, so a shopper (and Google, and AI assistants) can tell two bags apart from the name alone.

**Pattern: [what it is] + [the one detail no other product has] + [type word]**

Examples: *Frog in overalls phone bag* · *Big-mouth frog in a hooded jacket* · *Flip-mouth frog phone bag*.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the character or motif.** That is what people remember and search for: frog, schnauzer, sunflower.
2. **Add one detail that only this product has.** Take it from the product's own description, never invent it: *in overalls*, *hooded jacket*, *lollipop belly*, *odd-eyed*.
3. **End with a type word from this list only:**
   - **phone bag:** slim crossbody sized for a phone
   - **crossbody bag** or **bag:** bigger character or flower bags
   - **purse:** small bags called purses on the listing
   - **mini bag**, **shoulder bag**, **tote**, **backpack**, **kiss-lock bag**
   - **coasters**, **mug rugs**, **hat**
4. **Name the choices when they are the point:** *Plush schnauzer, puppy or terrier phone bag*. Do this for at most three choices.
5. **Packs say the count:** *set of 4*, *set of 5*.
6. **Say who it is for only when it is only for them:** *for kids*.
7. **Keep it short:** under 50 characters where possible. Use sentence case, and do not put "crochet" in the name. The page title (seoTitle) already says "crochet" and "handmade".
8. **No two names may share the same first two words plus type word.** The tests check that every name is unique.
9. **Do not change the web address (the slug)** when a name changes. The old address keeps its Google ranking.

## New product checklist

- What is it? Character or motif.
- What does it have that no other listing has?
- Which type word fits?
- Read the name next to the shop's other names in the same group. Could a shopper mix them up?
